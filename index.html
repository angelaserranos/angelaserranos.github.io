During my bachelor studies, I have used Abaqus to perform finite element analysis (FEA) on different structures. Pictures show a simplified design of a commercial aircraft wing box, in this project we worked with the application of different boundary conditions and distributed or punctual loads to simulate the effect of loads like lift or the engines weight, respectively.

This project also covered defining material properties and assigning different materials like Aluminium, Titanium or composite materials (CFRP) to each section of the structure to balance weight reduction and structural strength. Simulations were run iteratively to analyze stress distributions and maximum displacements in order to ensure structural integrity and compliance with the defined requirements.

Design and Optimisation of a Hydraulic Brake

Bachelor Thesis (TFG)

In the aerospace and heavy machinery industries, hydraulic brakes are essential due to their ability to transmit very high forces and their quick responsiveness. In this project, I wanted to tackle the limitation of hydraulic brakes strongly relying on trial-and-error design processes, involving the manufacturing of physical prototypes, by using CFD analysis and a digital modelling approach.

Geometry & Meshing

I designed and analyzed a preliminary hydraulic brake model, using Gmsh for the geometry and mesh generation and the open-source CFD software OpenFOAM for the simulations. Gmsh tasks were automated to study different geometrical configurations and mesh refinements. A mesh convergence study was performed in order to determine the most appropriate mesh giving representative results without added computational costs.

In order to also reduce the computational cost of the simulation, those regions with a smaller impact on the result had a coarser mesh. This required to also work with the compatibility of mesh elements between regions and to evaluate mesh quality metrics such as the aspect ratio, non-orthogonality or skewness.

Simulation Setup & Automation

Since previous studies showed that the transient part of the process could be neglected, simulations were run using the SimpleFoam solver. SimpleFoam is a steady-state solver for incompressible and turbulent flow that uses the SIMPLE (Semi-Implicit Method for Pressure Linked Equations) algorithm.

Pressure and viscous forces exerted by the fluid on the brake's internal walls were extracted and integrated into an analytical model to simulate the braking profiles for different punctual masses. The analytical model was built using MATLAB, considering the braking force evolution with displacement and using MATLAB's ode45 solver with custom event functions to integrate Newton's Second Law. A numerical integration approach was also used to estimate the thermal dissipation during the braking process.

Beyond the CFD setup, a significant part of this project involved programming and workflow automation. MATLAB was also used to dynamically compute the geometric coordinates for the different configurations and I wrote an Allrun executable bash script to fully automate the OpenFOAM execution pipeline.

Findings & Conclusions

Findings revealed that the model overestimated performance for lighter masses, causing large and potentially damaging decelerations, while underestimated it for heavy ones. The most appropriate balance between braking effectiveness and resource utilization was found for intermediate masses.

Beyond the numerical results themselves, this project allowed me to develop a fully automated analysis pipeline that can be extrapolated to more complex and realistic models and also to move beyond predefined OpenFoam tutorials.

Environmental Research on Persistent Pollutants

IES Arquitecto Ventura Rodríguez — High School Research Project

This project evaluated the presence of polybrominated diphenyl ethers (PBDEs) and polychlorinated dibenzo-p-dioxins and dibenzofurans (PCDD/Fs) in Spanish sewage sludge. These compounds are classified as Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs), chemical substances that resist environmental degradation, accumulate through food chains, and are widely embedded in everyday products such as industrial flame retardants or household goods.

Data from different autonomous communities was evaluated to study the effect of different treatment methods in the pollutant levels. The use of the Shapiro-Wilk text revealed that the analysed samples did not adjust to normal distributions. Therefore, non-parametrical methods were used.

The U-Mann-Whitney test was used to find statistical differences between Wastewater Treatment Plants (WWTPs) using anaerobic and aerobic digesters.

Results concluded that the use and production of these compounds has kept stable during the last years. No significant differences (neither increase nor decrease) in the concentrations were found based on the treatment method, suggesting that neither PBDEs nor PCDD/Fs are susceptible to increase or decrease their presence by the use of anaerobic or aerobic digesters.

This project was my first contact with academia, requiring me to search for useful research papers related to the topic and figure out how to apply the different statistical methods to my data. Although it was a bit confusing at the beginning, it was a very enriching experience to increase my knowledge about a new topic and improve my problem-solving skills.
